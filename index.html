Research

I work on machine learning for network traffic monitoring and analysis, and on secure, scalable IoT — the two halves of a doctorate completed at the University of Salamanca under Juan M. Corchado and Javier Prieto, within the EU Horizon 2020 IoTalentum training network.

On the monitoring side: knowledge distillation, ensemble methods and self-supervised representation learning for traffic classification and anomaly detection. On the systems side: two consensus protocols designed for devices that cannot afford proof of work — Proof-of-Resource and an aging-based proof of stake.

More recently I have been working on language models as agents over live measurement, and on neuro-symbolic architectures for intent-based networking, where a symbolic layer constrains what the model is permitted to assert.

Software

agentic-observability

A network monitor that reports the limits of its own instrument. Before it will call a change real, it measures that path's noise floor from the path's own history and asks whether a change of that size could have been seen at all — so “nothing happened” is never returned when the truth is “I could not have observed it.” Unmeasured time is never rendered as healthy time.

Verification lives in code rather than in the prompt: every claim the model makes is checked against the same statistics the monitor alerts on, and the check is appended to the answer instead of replacing it, so disagreement stays visible.

10.50.16.1/rtt_avg_ms — could I detect a 10% change?
instrument floor : steps of 0.1 on a median of 2 → nothing below 5.0% is expressible
statistical floor: 75% at 80% power
binding limit   : STATISTICS, at 75.0%
VERDICT: NO. A “no change” result here would mean “invisible”, NOT “nothing happened”.
